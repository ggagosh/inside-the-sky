Art brief · for paintings on paper · working title

Inside the Sky

A strategy game for the computer. The player leads a secret underground in a grey city where radio towers keep everyone loyal. The game needs your paintings: a city map, portraits of the people, scenes from the story, and small icons. This page explains what to paint, at what size, and how to send it.

Inspired by the Strugatsky brothers' novel The Inhabited Island. Names, places and designs are our own. Please don't use the 2008 film, existing illustrations of the book or earlier games based on it as references.

Start here

You don't need to know anything about games. Paint on paper, in your own style. For a start we need just three small things. When they arrive, we put them into the game and send you a screenshot, so you see how your work looks there before doing the rest.

Test 1

A portrait of Max

  • Head and shoulders, turned slightly to the side.
  • Plain, light background.
  • Frame size and eye line are shown under "Sizes on paper".
Test 2

A pencil sketch of the city

  • Rough pencil only, no painting yet.
  • The city seen from high above, with the 8 districts from the layout below.
  • We agree on the layout first, then you paint it properly.
Test 3

Three small icons

  • Money, a safe house, a tower.
  • Ink on white paper, each in a 5 × 5 cm square.
  • They'll be shown very small, so simple and bold is best.

Then scan or photograph them as described under "Sending your work", and send the original files.

The story in short

A grey capital is ruled by the Nameless Fathers, whom nobody has ever seen. Their Shield Towers are officially missile defense. Really, twice a day, they send out a pulse that fills everyone with happy loyalty. During the pulse, the whole city freezes, faces turned up toward the towers. A few people, the deviants, don't feel the bliss. They feel terrible pain instead, and the secret police hunt them as traitors.

The player leads a small underground of deviants. Max, a stranger who arrived from somewhere far away, joins them. The pulse does nothing to him at all. Together they grow a secret network across the city, compete with a rival group that wants the towers for itself, and try to bring the towers down.

The mood

Dark, grey, cold and quiet. People are afraid and watch each other. Small moments of human warmth, like a café or a friend, matter because they are rare. Think of a city in the 1940s after a long war: damp concrete, rust, trams, long coats, peeling posters.

  1. The sky glows. No sun, no moon, no stars. By day the whole sky is a pale, milky haze; at night it is a dark ceiling with no stars. Light comes from everywhere at once, so daylight has no strong shadows, only soft dark areas under things. At night, lamps and searchlights can throw hard local shadows.
  2. The far land curves up. People here believe they live on the inside of a ball, and their world looks like it: far-away land rises toward the top of the view and fades into the haze, as if the city sits at the bottom of a bowl. Show this at the top of the city map and in outdoor scenes.
  3. Towers are always in sight. Tall steel lattice towers, many shaped like a narrow waist (a hyperboloid), each with a ring on top. Every outdoor scene shows at least one.
  4. Gold belongs to the regime. Only towers, badges and the Fathers' emblem get the gold-yellow color. Everything else stays grey, brown and dull.

Paints and colors

Use whatever you work best in: charcoal, pencil, ink wash, watercolor, gouache. A small, muted palette suits the game. These are suggestions in ordinary paint names.

HazeWhite with a touch of raw umber. Day sky.
SlatePayne's grey. Shadows, distance, night.
SootIvory black or charcoal. Iron, coats, dark lines.
MudRaw umber. Walls, ground, wood.
RustBurnt sienna. Bricks, old metal, blood.
Regime goldYellow ochre. Towers and badges only.
Important for scanning

Please don't use metallic gold or silver paint, glitter or glossy gel pens. They reflect light and come out as white or black blotches in a scan or photo. A plain yellow ochre reads as gold on screen.

The city map

The most important painting. The player looks at it all game long. Paint the capital from high above at an angle, like an old bird's-eye city view, as if seen from the top of a tower. At the top edge, the far land rises into the haze (rule 2). Below is the layout of districts. It's a plan, not a style: change shapes freely, but keep the 8 districts, the river and the bridges.

  • Give every district one landmark the player can recognize at a glance: the big tower, the university dome, the palace, the barracks yard, cranes at the docks, the café square, chimneys, the train sheds.
  • Make the district borders visible: streets, walls, the river, changes in roofs. We'll trace them in the game so the player can click each district.
  • No text on the map. The game adds names, so they can change later.

The people

13 portraits: 9 named characters and 4 ordinary recruits. The "based on" line names the book character, if you want to read for personality. Max is the odd one out: healthier, taller and calmer than anyone in this city.

The underground (the player's side)

Max

based on Maxim Kammerer

Early twenties, tall, strong, open face, short light hair. Wears a borrowed local coat that is too small for him. He looks too healthy for this place. The pulse doesn't affect him at all.

Vep

based on Vepr

Leader of the underground. Middle-aged, thin, one arm, the empty sleeve pinned up. Short grey hair, calm, cold, clever eyes. Shabby long coat.

Odri

based on Ordi Tader

Saboteur, mid-twenties. Sharp face, restless, a worker's cap and jacket. Small burn scars on her fingers from handling explosives.

Zev

based on Zef

Forger and printer, in his forties. Big, wild red beard, messy hair, ink-stained hands, cracked glasses. A former scholar who laughs loudly at bad moments.

Rada

based on Rada Gaal

Early twenties, works at a café in the Old Town. Kind, tired face, hair under a scarf, apron over a cardigan. Her brother serves in the Guard.

The other side

Kai

based on Gai Gaal

Rada's brother, about twenty, a young guardsman who truly believes. Neat olive uniform, black beret, polished gold badge.

Captain Chako

based on Captain Chachu

The Guard officer who leads the raids. Thickset, scarred, loud. Greatcoat, officer's cap, gold badges.

The Stranger

based on the Wanderer

Head of the secret police. Bald, tall, thin, dark round glasses, a plain dark suit. Calm and impossible to read. Keep part of his face in shadow.

Kessel

new character

Leader of the rival underground group. In his fifties, a polished former officer: trim moustache, good coat with a fur collar, a signet ring, a charming smile that doesn't reach his eyes.

Ordinary recruits

Four plain faces the player can recruit: a factory worker (man, cap), a student (young, glasses, scarf), a nurse (woman, plain white headscarf), a tram driver (older man, uniform cap, moustache).

Icons

Small pictures for money, actions and map markers. Ink on white paper, one in each 5 × 5 cm square. They'll appear about as big as a fingernail on screen, so: thick lines, simple shapes, no fine detail.

The Fathers' emblemInvent it: something built from a tower and a ring of light. It appears on badges, posters and official papers.

Sending your work

Best: a scanner

  • Scan in color at 300 dpi and save as JPEG at high quality, with automatic "enhance" or "fix colors" turned off.
  • The map is A3, too big for most home scanners. A copy shop can scan A3 cheaply. Otherwise, photograph it.

Fine: a phone photo

  • Daylight from a window, but not direct sun. No flash.
  • Paper lying flat. Not under glass or plastic, which reflects.
  • Hold the phone straight above the paper, parallel to it, so the frame isn't skewed. Watch that the phone's shadow doesn't fall on the painting.
  • Fill the photo with the painting, edges included.
How to send

Send the original files by email, Google Drive or Telegram "as a file". WhatsApp, Viber and normal Telegram photos shrink and blur the image. We do all the cleanup after that: cropping, color, removing the paper background from icons.

How we work together

  1. Test set first: Max's portrait, the pencil layout and three icons.

  2. We put them into the game and send you a screenshot, so you see your work on screen at its real size.

  3. We agree on any changes once, then you continue with P1, then P2.

  4. Send pieces as you finish them. Each goes into the game as it arrives.
